# DOA Estimation: A Non-Convex Joint calibration and Spectral Analysis Method [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction: In many scientific and engineering fields, improving the performance of measurement tools, including radar, microscopes, and crystallography, is of great interest. However, due to variations in temperature, pressure, and humidity during manufacturing or even during the measurement process, the performance or sensitivity of each sensor in a measurement tool may deviate from the specified specifications or during the measurement process. Therefore, it is necessary to calibrate sensor parameters as well as recover the desired signals from collected measurements simultaneously, to ensure accurate signal measurement performance in measurement tools, especially in sensor arrays.
Direction of Arrival (DoA) Estimation [1, 2, 3] is a well-known problem in the field of signal processing. Its purpose is to estimate the direction of the impact signal using the measured values collected from a sensor array. Due to the relationship between the beam pattern introduced by Fourier transform and the array excitation, DoA estimation can be considered as a spectral estimation problem where the spectrum corresponds to the direction of arrival.
Different from traditional DoA (Direction of Arrival) problems, blind phase and gain calibration with spectrum estimation (BPGC-SE) simultaneously recovers unknown sensor calibration parameters as well as the frequency locations of the desired signals [4,5,6,7]. As each sensor is assumed to suffer from unknown gain and phase perturbations estimated, BPGC-SE becomes more challenging than traditional DoA estimation problems. In this regard, the authors in [7] proposed an alternating method that iteratively updates sensor parameters by fixing frequencies, and then iteratively updates frequencies by fixing sensor parameters until a specific predefined tolerance is met. However, due to the alternating nature of this method, it may suffer from error propagation. In [5,6], the authors proposed an algebraic approach assuming the source is uncorrelated; thus, the covariance matrix measured in a perfectly calibrated array is a Toeplitz matrix. By leveraging the Toeplitz structure, they obtained a linear equation system and solved it to recover the sensor parameters ignoring phase wrapping. Additionally, the authors in [4] proposed estimating both sensor parameters and the Toeplitz covariance matrix via gradient descent minimization of a non-linear least square loss function with regularization terms using Wirtinger iterations. However, this algorithm contains many regularization parameters that need careful tuning.
In this paper, we study the BPGC (Blind Phase and Gain Correction) estimation problem, which is a nonlinear issue with unknown sensor parameters and signal parameters. Unlike previous works that directly attempt to estimate sensor parameters, our goal is to estimate sensor parameters through inverse quantities that perfectly calibrate the measured values when multiplied by the sensor measurements. This allows us to transform the nonlinear sensor calibration problem into a linear one under certain rank-half positive definite matrices and a semi-positive definite Toeplitz matrix. We then propose a non-convex minimization method to find factors of rank-one matrices under unit norm constraints, aiming to avoid trivial solutions. The problem is solved using the trust-region method [8, 9, 10]. Numerical experiments show that our proposed non-convex optimization method outperforms other known methods [7, 6, 4] in various aspects, including frequency separation restrictions, noise sensitivity, and scaling with the number of snapshots.

In a broader context, the BPGC problem has been widely studied in recent literature, with different assumptions made. Our paper argues for the use of multiple snapshot measurements and their covariance structure. In contrast, recent works have imposed additional structural assumptions on sensor parameters, such as sparsity or subspace priors, by considering only single measurement vectors [11, 12, 13, 14]. Additionally, the setting of multiple snapshots without spectral estimation has been considered in [13, 15, 16].

Conclusion: This paper investigates the blind phase and gain correction problem with direction of arrival estimation. Under the assumption of uncorrelated sources, the covariance matrix of measurements obtained from uniform array sensors is a Toeplitz matrix. By introducing an inverse calibration vector, the blind phase and gain correction problem is transformed into a linear problem on a rank-one semi-positive definite matrix. Then, we propose a non-convex minimization method to find the inverse calibration parameters by considering the null space of the Toeplitz matrix under unit norm constraints, aiming to avoid trivial solutions. Numerical experiments demonstrate that our proposed non-convex method outperforms existing methods, including algebraic methods [6], optimization WF [4], and alternating methods [7], without tuning parameters.
